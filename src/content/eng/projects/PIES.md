**PIES** (*Prüm Implementation, Evaluation, and Strengthening of Forensic DNA Data Exchange*) was a European project dedicated to implementing, evaluating and strengthening **forensic DNA** data exchange under **Prüm**.

## Research question

How can cooperation between institutions exchanging forensic DNA data be improved, and how can these exchanges be better used for research and forensic intelligence? The project combined the study of exchange practices with data analysis, particularly to explore cross-border crime.

## My contribution

I **co-authored and submitted the funding proposal**, then served as **coordinator and principal investigator**. At the [NICC](/eng/nicc/), I coordinated the consortium and led the project scientifically throughout its duration.

## Consortium

The consortium brought together **seven institutions in four countries**: the NICC in Belgium; the Sous-Direction de la Police Technique et Scientifique in France; the Nederlands Forensisch Instituut, Nederlands Studiecentrum Criminaliteit en Rechtshandhaving and Leiden University in the Netherlands; Northumbria University and Sustainable Criminal Justice Solutions in the United Kingdom. **Europol** also participated as a partner that received no funding.

This cooperation combined expertise in forensic science, criminology and judicial cooperation around DNA data exchange and use.

## Works and outputs

My contributions include technical monitoring and final reports, as well as presentations on international cooperation and the use of DNA data to study organized or cross-border crime.

Two contributions provide an introduction to this work:

- the **[PIES Final technical implementation report](/eng/work/#publication-jeuniaux-2016-final-tech-report-pies)**, presenting the implementation of the project;
- the presentation **[Exploiting Forensic DNA data to draw pictures of organized or transnational crime(s)](/eng/work/#publication-jeuniaux-et-al-2015-stockholm-networks)**, on the use of DNA data to study these forms of crime.
