At the **University of Memphis** in the United States, I conducted my doctoral research in cognitive science within the **Institute for Intelligent Systems** and the **Department of Psychology**, particularly in Max Louwerse’s **Multimodal Aspects of Discourse** laboratory.

## Doctorate and language learning

I obtained a **PhD in experimental psychology, specialising in psycholinguistics**. My dissertation, **[The role of feedback in learning form-meaning mappings](/eng/work/#publication-jeuniaux-2009-feedback-dissertation)**, supervised by **Max Louwerse**, examined the role of feedback in learning relationships between forms and meanings.

This work combined the experimental study of language learning with data analysis and computational modelling.

## Cognition, discourse and communication

My research also addressed **discourse coherence**, semantic models such as **Latent Semantic Analysis**, **embodied cognition** and **multimodal communication**. In particular, I studied coordination between speakers and the relationships between language, perception and behaviour.

The experiments used audio and video recordings, mouse movement tracking and eye tracking, together with statistical and computational analyses.

## Teaching

In the Department of Psychology, I designed and taught two courses: **Introduction to Psychological Research** and **Thinking and Cognitive Processes**. They combined theoretical concepts with data analysis exercises in **R** and computerised assessments developed in **PHP** and **MySQL**.

## Related work

The article **[The linguistic and embodied nature of conceptual processing](/eng/work/#publication-louwerse-jeuniaux-2010-conceptual)** examines the linguistic and embodied dimensions of conceptual processing. **[Behavior Matching in Multimodal Communication Is Synchronized](/eng/work/#publication-louwerse-et-al-2012-multimodal)** addresses the synchronisation of behaviour in multimodal communication.
