## Some contributions

These works offer an overview of my research on language and cognition, forensic data and criminal justice trajectories. They were carried out with the colleagues listed in each reference.

<div class="stack-gap">

<div class="card">

### Evaluating language models

**Lenci et al. — [A comprehensive comparative evaluation and analysis of Distributional Semantic Models](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10579-021-09575-z)**

This study compares distributional semantic models, which represent meaning through word usage in corpora. It contributes to the evaluation of language representation methods at the intersection of linguistics and computational modelling.

</div>

<div class="card">

### Using relationships in DNA data

**Jeuniaux et al. — [Establishing networks in a forensic DNA database to gain operational and strategic intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1057/sj.2015.31)**

This work studies the construction of networks from a forensic DNA database to obtain operational and strategic intelligence. It illustrates the value of analysing relationships between data to use these resources at the scale of a network.

</div>

<div class="card">

### Studying recidivism through justice data

**Mine et al. — [Recidivism among people convicted of terrorism: A survival analysis based on Belgian Central Criminal Record](https://doi.org/10.1080/09546553.2025.2458241)**

This research uses data from the Belgian Central Criminal Record and survival analysis to study recidivism among people convicted of terrorism. It shows how administrative data can support the study of criminal justice trajectories.

</div>

<div class="card">

### Understanding multimodal communication

**Louwerse et al. — [Behavior Matching in Multimodal Communication Is Synchronized](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2012.01269.x)**

This study examines the synchronization of behaviour in multimodal communication. It forms part of my cognitive science research on coordination between people during their interactions.

</div>

<div class="card">

### Supporting decisions in complex systems

**Tremblay et al. — [A cognitive prosthesis for complex decision-making](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2016.07.009)**

This work examines a cognitive support system for complex decision-making. It connects the study of cognitive processes with the design and evaluation of tools intended to support human operators.

</div>
